WINNER (selected by AUC-PR, the primary metric for severe class imbalance): class_weighted
Justification: with fraud at ~1% of transactions, accuracy is nearly meaningless (predicting 'no fraud' for every transaction already scores ~99% accuracy), so AUC-PR (average precision) - which focuses on the precision/recall tradeoff for the minority (fraud) class - is the more decision-relevant metric. Class-weighting won here: it lets the model learn from the true data distribution and calibrate directly on real minority-class density, whereas SMOTE's synthetic interpolated fraud examples can blur decision boundaries - here the interpolated examples did not improve the ranking of real fraud in the unseen test months.

Honest discussion:
Synthetic dataset. An audit found that the first version of the generator leaked the label through the hour of day (hour 5 was 100% fraud; legitimate activity never happened at most night hours) and that the model used lifetime account aggregates computed over all 8 months including the fraud rows. Both were fixed: night-time activity is now a realistic skew, features are past-only, and evaluation is on unseen later months. The generator's signal level was tuned on a validation split inside the training months only. Real-world fraud is adversarial and noisier, so real AUC-PR would typically be lower still. Results are reported exactly as computed.
